# Five-minute demonstration

Lead with a practical question: **what do the published records show, and which service change deserves closer investigation?** Keep source dates, missing evidence and modeled assumptions visible throughout.

## Prepare

Follow [clone and run](clone-and-run.md), then rehearse at the actual presentation size and on a phone. The persistent navigation is **Analytics**, **Map & planning** and **Evidence**. Bundled analytical data does not require upstream portals during the demonstration; optional basemap tiles and live providers require connectivity.

Use the current values returned by the application. Do not recite results from an earlier dataset or model version. For forecast definitions and frozen receipts, use the [forecast issue workflow](forecast-issue-workflow.md) and [dated source receipt](prediction-source-receipt.md).

## 0:00–1:00 — Locate the evidence

On **Map & planning**, search for an island. Open its overview and explain the score drivers, service inventory and population reference year. Distinguish the island accessibility index from modeled resident journeys. Missing age, service or route evidence remains unavailable rather than becoming zero.

Show the national sea network. Its colors screen estimated service pressure using linked population and modeled scheduled departures. They do not measure passenger loads, congestion or the number of ferries required. Open a route to inspect its schedule basis and source.

## 1:00–2:00 — Inspect a journey

Search for a mapped place and open **Directions**. Select real endpoints, a travel mode and, for Bus & ferry, a supported date and time. Add a stop if useful. Trace the returned legs and waits; unavailable segments remain gaps.

Road directions use the mapped road graph. Published departures are dated evidence, and marine corridors are planning geometry. None of these represents live traffic or a vessel track. Refer to [map activity and resident access](engaged-map-and-planning.md) and [sea-service evidence](official-sea-services.md) for those distinctions.

## 2:00–3:15 — Compare a proposal

Create a supported bus or ferry proposal using **Map tools**. Read the quote, capital and annual costs, then add the proposal and compare **Without proposal** with **With proposal**. Show an improvement, a loss or an unknown result where the actual response supports it. Undo the proposal.

Walking access, expected waiting time and direct island connections are separate outcomes. More frequent service can reduce expected waiting without shortening the walk to a stop. Costs and carbon depend on the displayed assumptions; modeled residents gaining access are not measured riders or successful healthcare visits. Use the [proposal guide](proposal-experience.md) and [transport planning guide](map-transport-planning.md) for the current interaction.

## 3:15–4:15 — Make reliability visible

Under **Analytics → Passenger outlook**, inspect a target month, the trusted actual cutoff, the selected method and its simple benchmarks. Open later historical predictions and range coverage. Show an unfavorable comparison honestly.

Monthly passenger movements do not identify route occupancy or neighborhood demand. Revised-vintage historical checks are not prospective validation. Frozen issues preserve their original model/source version; later scores remain pending until comparable actuals are available.

## 4:15–5:00 — End with an inspectable next step

Open **Evidence** and identify the source, date and qualification behind one claim. Name a concrete next input: reviewed crossing runtimes, complete bus calendars, suitable facility entrances, observed loads or operator costs. Export a brief only when its assumptions and limitations support the question.

The [roadmap](roadmap.md) records the evidence gates. Developer checks do not replace a first-time policymaker study, operator review or rehearsal on the venue device. Recheck the [official competition FAQ](https://www.datathon.stats.gov.mv/faq) before submission; this demonstration makes no claim of organizer endorsement.
