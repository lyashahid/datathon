# Scenario presentation

Open **Map & planning**, then choose **Scenario** beside Directions. **Shift+P** starts the same presentation while the map is open; **Shift+S** remains an alias. The shortcuts ignore typing fields and repeated key events. No personal story or narration appears in the app.

Use the subtle **right arrow**, **Right arrow key**, **Page Down**, or **Space** to advance. Use the **left arrow**, **Left arrow key**, or **Page Up** to return. **R** replays a step. **Escape**, the close button, or the final arrow exits. The presentation advances only when the presenter chooses. The **To Hulhumalé** and **To the airport** journey clocks remain visible throughout the presentation and pause during explanatory steps. They display whole elapsed minutes, including days for journeys longer than 24 hours, with the current journey emphasized. The first includes the overnight ferry wait, crossings, intermediate waits, an explicitly assumed airport connection, and the estimated flight; the second includes walking, waiting, riding, and the terminal walk.

Five chapters organize sixteen manually controlled scenes: **Baarah**, **Ferry through the north**, **Hanimaadhoo to Velana**, **The Phase 3 access gap**, and **Journey to the airport**. These show Baarah’s access index, the ferry inspector and crossing to Vashafaru, Vashafaru’s access index, the ferry inspector and crossing to Dhidhdhoo, Dhidhdhoo’s access index, the ferry inspector and crossing to Hanimaadhoo, Hanimaadhoo’s airport details, the flight inspector and flight to Velana, northern Hulhumalé transport access, modeled population, the first walk, and the direct airport bus. There is no separate Malé step. The final scene ends at the airport; its arrow finishes the presentation. The island scenes reuse the app’s island access overview. Ferry facts stay alongside the island stats during travel, with larger crossing, frequency and wait figures; full source qualifications remain available in their disclosure. Ferry and flight inspectors retain their larger presentation typography. The flight marker uses the transparent generated PNG at `static/images/scenario-airplane.png`. Presentation data is kept separate from the saved planning draft.

The presentation fills the map workspace and hides its normal navigation until exit. Island details foreground one relevant fact: the access index, Hanimaadhoo’s airport, the modeled walk to a bus stop, or residents. **Connections, services & data** opens secondary native details. Advancing from a settled ferry inspector holds at the jetty during the captured wait, then follows the boat in the established view. Advancing from the settled flight inspector begins the assumed airport connection and then the flight. The camera follows moving vehicles continuously, while their markers move at subpixel precision. Replay establishes the route once. Arrival at Velana briefly frames the airport and Phase 3 together before focusing the neighbourhood. The access-to-population lens change keeps the same camera. Panning the map pauses the vehicle and clock; replay restarts the scene. Motion respects the reduced-motion setting.

The airport journey displays a walk/wait/ride breakdown. During the retained roughly 32-minute wait, the traveller remains at the boarding stop while the journey timer advances. The bus then follows the direct airport route. Presentation playback gives the wait, ride, and terminal walk roughly three, six, and one seconds respectively while preserving their actual modeled minutes. The three ferry waits receive 2.5, 0.8 and 1.8 seconds of screen time; the airport connection receives 2.5 seconds. Ferry and flight movement remains fast, at 4.5 and 4.75 seconds respectively. Visual acceleration never changes the displayed source minutes.

During ferry and flight travel, the departure island's access panel and heatmap stay visible through the wait and movement. At arrival, the stats, selected island, heatmap and coverage layers switch together to the destination while the travel scene remains paused on arrival. No additional click is needed to load that island. Pausing keeps the departure stats in place, and replay restores them until the vehicle arrives again. Ferry facts remain visible at arrival.

A small legend explains that transport heatmap colours describe walking access. Map focus and presentation styling are scoped to scenario mode; exiting restores normal navigation and the prior planning context.

Map startup prewarms profiles, connectivity, baseline data, and available access layers for Baarah, Vashafaru, Dhidhdhoo, Hanimaadhoo, Hulhumalé, and Malé. The presentation shares those active requests and keeps completed results for five minutes. Closing a presentation detaches its updates while prewarming continues. A failed section has a shorter fifteen-second retry cache, and available sections still appear immediately.

## Evidence used

- The demo starts in Baarah on **Saturday 10 October 2026 at 08:00**, after that morning’s 07:40 sailing. The retained timetable confirms the next departure on **Sunday 11 October**, so the initial wait is **23 hours 40 minutes**. Sequential queries follow that date: S1C5 Baarah **07:40 → Vashafaru 07:55** (**15 minutes**), S1C5 Vashafaru **08:00 → Dhidhdhoo 08:10** (**10 minutes**), and S1C6 Dhidhdhoo **11:00 → Hanimaadhoo 11:25** (**25 minutes**). The five-minute dwell and 170-minute connection wait are included. Total ferry elapsed time is **27 hours 25 minutes**. The source frequency is six weekly departures from each corridor’s start per direction; it is not observed sailings or a separately confirmed frequency for each leg. Their shoreline-aware route shapes come from the app’s existing model and are not navigation clearance. Next-sailing lookup is bounded to seven days; missing confirmation leaves downstream timing unknown.
- The HAQ–MLE route uses the existing bidirectional Maldivian connection and schematic air geometry. [Maldivian’s Hanimaadhoo destination page](https://maldivian.aero/destinations/hanimaadhoo.html) publishes an approximately **45-minute** flight from Malé; the presentation reuses that duration as an estimate in the reverse direction. The retained air record does not establish a dated HAQ–MLE timetable. The demo explicitly assumes **20 minutes harbour transfer plus 70 minutes check-in and connection allowance**, adding 90 minutes before the flight. [Maldivian’s check-in guidance](https://maldivian.aero/plan-and-book/online-check-in.html) supports arriving for boarding-pass collection and bag drop at least an hour before departure; it does not establish this demo’s 90-minute connection or a connecting flight. The combined presentation estimate is **29 hours 40 minutes**, displayed as **1d 5h 40m**. Actual flight waiting is unknown and may be longer. The clock ends at Velana, which serves Hulhumalé; it does not include onward travel into Hulhumalé. The animation is not a recorded flight track or a confirmed flight departure.
- The airport itinerary is an independent captured comparison using the retained **10 October 2026** bus service and a **15:20** departure from the modeled origin, boarding direct **R9** at Hiya Flats Stop 2 at **16:05** and arriving at VIA Domestic Terminal at **16:11**. It is not a dated connection to the Sunday ferry journey. Its **51.396-minute** clock includes **13.054 minutes initial walk, 31.946 minutes waiting, 6 minutes bus ride and 0.396 minutes terminal walk**, displayed as approximately 51 minutes. The MACL-to-airport display reverses the retained airport-side return corridor because the outbound source shape detours through Malé; this short display section is schematic because directional road access is incomplete. The other walk and bus geometry comes from the retained itinerary. The displayed route remains on Hulhumalé and the airport island and does not cross into Malé. The app does not infer a Phase 3 administrative boundary from the origin point.
- Access colors measure modeled walks to public transport. The population layer shows residents allocated by the existing model; it does not measure live bus occupancy, crowding, or traffic.
- The island access panels use the app’s retained profiles and coverage models. Missing resident coverage is shown as limited evidence and must not be interpreted as zero residents or zero travel time.

Sections load independently. If a source, timetable, or route cannot be confirmed, the presentation keeps the available sections and labels the missing result. Rain, traffic delays, and clinical travel requirements are not modeled.

## Generated airplane asset

The project asset `static/images/scenario-airplane.png` was created with the built-in image generation tool. The original transparent PNG is preserved in the project; the map decodes it into a smaller GPU texture for display. The marker is an illustration rather than an identified aircraft assignment.

The full generation prompt and saved asset details are recorded in [Airplane asset](scenario-airplane-asset.md).

## Browser verification

With the frontend and backend running, execute:

```powershell
$env:MOBILITY_QA_URL = 'http://localhost:5173'
node scripts/verify-scenario-presentation.mjs
```

The existing Playwright Chromium harness follows the sixteen scenes and five chapter states with reduced motion, then checks real vehicle motion, timer accumulation, stationary waiting, pausing by panning, replay, and exit. Its scene table checks the three ferry legs in order before HAQ–MLE. It covers the overnight and intermediate waits, departure stats during travel, destination stats and heatmaps at arrival without advancing, the assumed flight connection, hidden workspace chrome, visible whole-minute journey clocks and their active state, readable route metrics, keyboard access to secondary island details, recovery after tab visibility changes, unchanged access-to-population framing, restoration of the saved planning draft, and delayed ferry timing updates. Desktop, tablet, and phone screenshots and `verification.json` are written to `artifacts/qa/scenario-presentation/`. A failed or slow check also captures a diagnostic screenshot. Set `MOBILITY_QA_CHECK` to a check-name substring for a focused run; for example, `Airport arrival` checks the route order, both presentation shortcuts, and the two ways to finish without waiting through travel playback. A focused run writes `verification-targeted.json`.
